## 1. Research Objectives

1. **Understand Devotee & Family Pain Points**: Identify the emotional, physical, and informational friction points during temple pilgrimages (specifically in the Mount Abu – Ambaji corridor).
2. **Validate Digital Trust Factors**: Determine what visual cues, source tags, and content formats convey authentic trust to pilgrims vs. tourists.
3. **Analyze Local Merchant Workflows**: Unpack how taxi operators, dharamshala managers, and local guides manage bookings, handle customer enquiries, and navigate digital tools.
4. **Identify Accessibility Barriers**: Benchmark the physical terrain realities (steps, climbs, wheelchairs, weather) and assess how digital alerts can prevent on-site distress.

## 2. Target Participant Segments

| Cohort | Sample Size | Demographic Profile | Target Research Context |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **Devout Elders & Pilgrims** | 12 | Age 55+, Gujarati/Hindi speakers, travelling with multi-generational families. | Ambaji darshan, Gabbar Hill climb, dharamshala booking, dietary needs. |
| **Cultural & Solo Travelers** | 8 | Age 22–35, tech-savvy, English/Hindi speakers, budget/mid-range travelers. | Mount Abu sightseeing, trekking (Guru Shikhar), heritage guides, local transit. |
| **Local Service Operators** | 6 | Age 30–50, taxi owners, local hotel/ashram managers in Abu Road & Ambaji. | Lead generation, phone enquiries, local commission structures. |

---

## 3. Methodologies & Study Cadence

* **Contextual Inquiries & Shadowing**: Observing family groups navigating temple entry gates, shoe-drop counters, and queue lines in Ambaji and Dilwara.
* **Semi-Structured Interviews**: 45-minute remote/in-person interviews focusing on travel planning habits, fear of touts, and language preferences.
* **Card Sorting & Navigation Tree Testing**: Testing information architecture categories (e.g., "Pilgrimage" vs. "Sightseeing", "Ritual Timings" vs. "Darshan Guidelines").
* **Usability Benchmarking**: Moderated prototype testing on mid-tier Android devices running Hindi and Gujarati localized versions.
